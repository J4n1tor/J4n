`py` runs Python in a kernel that keeps its variables for this task. `import j4n`. Use code for what the data fixes (parse, count, filter, sort, join): `j4n.chunks(text, n)` cuts a long text at its paragraph breaks, `j4n.table(text)` reads a markdown or CSV table as dicts, `j4n.grep(items, pattern)` gives the indexes of exact matches, `j4n.dedup(items)` drops repeats, and `j4n.heads(items)` shows each piece's headings and first lines. For yes/no, a pick or a score over many items, `j4n.judge(items, question)` gives {index: p}, `j4n.choose(items, question, options)` and `j4n.score(items, question, levels)` do the other two: each answers a whole list in about a second, and `j4n.keep(p)` lists the indexes not clearly ruled out. They work only where the project has turned on `kit.judge`; the error says so. `j4n.model(prompt, context=kept)` asks a chat model last, on what was kept. Read the result, not the data: print counts and the kept items. `j4n.recipes()` lists ready programs; `j4n.recipe(name, ...)` runs one. `j4n.call(name, **args)` calls any of your other tools from code, under the same rules, and gives back its text. `j4n.tools()` lists them and `help(j4n)` shows the rest.
